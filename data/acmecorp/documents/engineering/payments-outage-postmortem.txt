Postmortem: Payments Outage of 18 August 2026
Owner: Engineering, Payments team
Status: Final
Audience: Engineering

1. Summary

On 18 August 2026 the payments service rejected or timed out on most requests for 47 minutes, from 10:42 to 11:29 CET. About 3,900 customer payments failed during that time. No payment was charged twice and no data was lost.

2. Impact

- 47 minutes of degraded service.
- Roughly 3,900 failed payment attempts. About 70 percent were retried successfully by customers within the hour.
- 212 support tickets.

3. Root cause

The root cause was connection pool exhaustion in the payments service.

A release at 10:31 changed the fraud scoring call from asynchronous to synchronous. Each payment request now held a database connection while it waited for the fraud scoring service. Under normal load this added about 40 ms and went unnoticed. At 10:42 the fraud scoring service slowed down, the waiting requests held their connections for several seconds each, and the pool of 50 connections ran out. New requests waited for a connection and then timed out.

4. Why it was not caught

- The canary stage passed, because the fraud scoring service was fast during those 15 minutes.
- There was no alert on connection pool usage, only on error rate.
- The load test in staging uses a stub for fraud scoring that always answers in 5 ms.

5. Timeline (CET)

10:31  Release reaches full rollout.
10:42  Fraud scoring latency rises. Payment error rate starts to climb.
10:46  Error rate alert pages the on-call engineer.
10:58  On-call engineer suspects the database and fails over to the replica. No improvement.
11:17  Connection pool exhaustion identified from service metrics.
11:24  Manual rollback of the 10:31 release started.
11:29  Error rate back to normal.

6. What went well

The rollback itself took 5 minutes. No duplicate charges occurred, because payment requests carry an idempotency key.

7. What went badly

It took 31 minutes from the page to the right diagnosis. The failover to the replica cost time and was not needed.

8. Actions

- Release the database connection before calling fraud scoring. Owner: Payments team. Done 20 August.
- Add an alert when connection pool usage is above 80 percent for 2 minutes. Owner: Platform team. Done 25 August.
- Add a 300 ms timeout and a fallback to the fraud scoring call. Owner: Payments team. Due 30 September.
- Make the staging stub for fraud scoring return realistic latency. Owner: Platform team. Due 15 October.
